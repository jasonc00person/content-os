## Who buys

**Primary: operators who film themselves to sell their own thing.** Coaches, experts, local and trade businesses, founders with a product, in-house marketers. Time-poor, usually 30+, often outside the US, on a Mac with a $20-$100 Claude plan, beginner-to-intermediate with AI. Their footage piles up because editing eats hours. They want to post every week so customers see them. **Going viral is not their goal; getting customers is.** They retain best.

**Secondary:** content service providers (agencies, freelance editors, growth operators): loud, high-earning, fastest to churn. Early creators with a day job and no offer yet: retain well, most price-sensitive.

**Not for:** freebie seekers, broke beginners, people who want a magic button, tinkerers who fork the files and leave.

**The front door is the Claude Video Editor.** Editing is the top pain and the top reason people join. Their first objection is "does it run on my editor, my OS, my Claude plan, and what will the Claude bill be," not the Skool price.

## How content turns into members

**Demo → YouTube deep-dive → Skool.**
- **Instagram / short form** demos a real system running (the output and the speed, never a claim). It never teaches the full build. CTA = comment a keyword, the DM sends the YouTube video or resource.
- **YouTube long form** teaches the system and is the signup engine. Topic choice on YouTube is a revenue decision.
- **The Skool** sells the shortcut: the files, the calls, the support.

One CTA everywhere: join the Skool.

## Who counts as a competitor

Use this filter when scouting creators (feed passes, `competitor-list.md`):

**In:** AI-related, educational creators: they teach AI tools (Claude, Claude Code, ChatGPT, agents, automations, AI video/image workflows) and show their own working setups, aimed at people who build businesses and content, ideally selling a community or course. The benchmark is Nick Saraev: AI education that teaches something real. Bonus points for AI video editing, content systems, or "one person runs a whole business with AI."

**Out:** anything not about AI, even if it's business or content education (content strategy, personal branding, sales tactics with no AI in them). Also out: AI lifestyle posts and shitposts that teach nothing, pure AI news recaps, crypto/trading, generic hustle/motivation, dropshipping and "make money online." (Jason, 2026-11-23: "we're looking for AI related stuff, educational stuff too, not just lifestyle or random shitposts.")

**What "killing it" means here:** a creator whose recent reels pull well above their own follower count (outliers), not just a big account. New and fast-rising beats old and big.
