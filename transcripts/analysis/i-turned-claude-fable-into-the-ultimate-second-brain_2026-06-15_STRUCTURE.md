## Beat-by-Beat Structural Map

### 1. Cold Open / Hook — [0:00 – 0:13]
**Function:** Stop the scroll with a big claim + intrigue. Establishes scale ("entire life and business in one place") and plants the payoff promise (automation).
**Content:** "This right here is basically my entire life and my entire business all in one second brain. And Claude Fable understands all of this better than I do — and it's helping me automate a lot of this."

### 2. News Peg / Relevance Hook — [0:13 – 0:46]
**Function:** Attach the video to a fresh trending event (model launch) so it feels timely and clickable; borrows authority from named experts.
**Content:** Claude Fable just dropped — basically Claude Mythos 5 with extra cyber guardrails. Name-drops Karpathy & Boris Cherny + "the community" calling it a major step.

### 3. Urgency / Stakes — [0:46 – 1:15]
**Function:** Manufacture scarcity (limited window) and set a cost expectation that doubles as proof of power.
**Content:** Only available on subscription June 9–22, then switches to usage credits. It's 2x Opus pricing ($10/M in, $50/M out) — "it'll eat your subscription faster."

### 4. Credibility / Context-Setting — [1:15 – 1:50]
**Function:** Establish authority and pre-empt the "you just built this for the hype" objection. Sets the "I" of the system (Herk 2).
**Content:** "This is my second brain, and my OS is called Herk 2. I didn't build this today — I've been working on it for months. But today I played with Fable all day to see how it slots in."

### 5. Promise / Roadmap — [1:50 – 2:05]
**Function:** Tell the viewer exactly what they'll get, lowering bounce risk by setting expectations.
**Content:** "I'll show you exactly how I set everything up, how I keep improving it, and how I'm so productive and efficient."

### 6. Reframe — "It's a Mindset Shift" — [2:05 – 2:40]
**Function:** Lower the technical barrier; reframe the topic from "tool" to "habit," making it accessible to non-coders. Relatable confession builds rapport.
**Content:** A lot of this is an adoption/habits shift. Before, he juggled tabs, custom GPTs, repeated himself — productive but no real "co-founder." Step one: close the tabs.

### 7. Core Principle Drop #1 — [2:40 – 2:50]
**Function:** Deliver a sticky, quotable maxim that anchors the philosophy. Tweetable soundbite.
**Content:** "An OS doesn't start with architecture. It starts with a default" (default to Claude Code / your harness for everything).

### 8. Conceptual Foundation — Two Layers — [2:50 – 3:35]
**Function:** Build the mental model before the framework; define terms so the rest of the video has scaffolding.
**Content:** Two layers: Second Brain (your knowledge — does it know your life/business?) sits underneath the AI Operating System (skills/automation built on top). You can't have the OS without the brain.

**Function:** The spine of the video. Present the named, ordered framework that makes the content feel proprietary and teachable.
**Content:** Four C's in order: **Context** (who you/your business are) → **Connections** (live data) → **Capabilities** (skills/agents/automations) → **Cadence** (runs on its own). First two = second brain; last two = the OS. Frames it as something you could teach a team or sell to a business.

### 10. Body Section 1 — Context (the "Routing Tree") — [4:50 – 6:06]
**Function:** Deliver the first C's value; teach the core concept (CLAUDE.md as router) and pre-answer a top community question ("when is it too much context?").
**Content:** CLAUDE.md is a router pointing the agent to rules, references, skills, projects, wikis. Pulse check = can you AND the agent find files fast? If it searches 5 min for a file you know, fix the architecture.

### 11. Demo / Over-the-Shoulder #1 — Context Setup Tour — [6:06 – 9:55]
**Function:** Proof-by-showing. Convert the abstract framework into concrete screen reality; the "other worlds" tip + token-count reassurance answer real audience anxieties.

## Script Skeleton (Reusable Template)

> A long-form "framework + over-the-shoulder demo" teach script, pegged to a trending event.

1. **Cold Open Hook** — One bold claim about the result your system delivers + a teaser of the magic.
2. **News Peg** — Tie to a fresh, trending event in your niche; borrow authority by name-dropping respected voices.
3. **Urgency / Stakes** — A time window, price, or cost that makes acting now feel necessary (and signals power).
4. **Credibility** — "This isn't new for me — I've been doing it for months." Pre-empt the hype-chaser objection.
5. **Promise / Roadmap** — State plainly what they'll learn.
6. **Reframe** — Lower the barrier: "this is a [mindset/habit] shift, not a [technical] one." Add a relatable before-state confession.
7. **Quotable Maxim #1** — A short, tweetable principle that anchors the philosophy.
8. **Conceptual Foundation** — Define your core terms / mental model before the framework.
9. **Framework Reveal** — Your named, ordered framework (the proprietary spine). Frame it as teachable/sellable.
10. **Body Section 1..N (one per framework element)** — For each: teach the principle → pre-answer the top audience question about it.
11. **Demo / Over-the-Shoulder** — Show the real thing on screen. Include an honest correction to build trust.
12. **Mid-Roll Soft CTA** — Offer the deeper free resource (course/community/repo) as added value, framed against "this video is just the high level."
13. **Payoff Demos (escalating)** — 1-2 "wow" outputs, each bigger than the last. Tie each back to the framework.
14. **Authority Insert** — A third-party quote/tweet validating your core claim; stay balanced by naming the downsides too.
15. **Tension Peak / Cautionary Tale** — Your most memorable story (ideally a costly failure) that teaches a hard-won principle. Quotable Maxim #2 lands here.
16. **Rapid Value Stack** — 3 practical, immediately-usable tips for viewers who stayed.
17. **Closing Thesis** — Zoom out to the durable, reframing takeaway that removes the viewer's anxiety/FOMO.
18. **FAQ / Lightning Round** — Sweep up buyer/skeptic objections; slip in the final CTA.
19. **Outro** — Like ask + gratitude + next-video loop.

## Structural Notes

- **Triple-stacked hook in the first 75 seconds.** Bold claim (0:00) → news peg (0:13) → urgency/scarcity (0:46) all fire before any teaching. The trending model launch does the click-work; the framework does the watch-time work. The actual hook is one sentence — everything after it is escalation.

- **Demos are deliberately delayed and escalated, not front-loaded.** The two "wow" payoffs (explainer video at 11:55, relationship map at 14:28) sit at the ~⅓ and ~½ marks — placed to rescue retention right when viewers typically drop, and ordered smallest→biggest. Earlier sections (Context/Connections) are "trust the framework"; the demos are the reward for staying.

- **Honest corrections are a retention/trust tactic, not accidents.** He flags the wrong subscriber count (12:11) and Fable's downsides (21:22) on purpose — credibility-through-vulnerability that pre-empts skeptics and doubles as a re-teach of static-vs-live data.

- **Transitions are signposted by the framework itself.** "Moving on to the third C, capabilities…" The named, ordered Four C's act as built-in chapter markers, so the viewer always knows where they are and how much is left — a structural retention device that needs no scripted segue.

- **CTAs are soft, value-framed, and split across the runtime.** No hard pitch. The mid-roll (10:50) and FAQ (31:19) CTAs both point to the same free Skool/repo and are framed as "this video is the mindset, the course is the step-by-step" — so the offer reads as generosity. The only hard ask (like the video) waits until the very end.

- **The emotional peak is a failure story, placed late.** The "$150K–200K wrong email" cautionary tale (24:26) is the most vivid beat and lands deep in the back half, right where a pure how-to would sag — converting a dry "permission layer" lesson into a memorable, shareable moment ("keys, not prompts").
