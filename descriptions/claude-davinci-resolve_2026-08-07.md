Summary ⤵️

Claude can now take full control of DaVinci Resolve. It cuts your raw footage, builds the motion graphics, drops in title cards, color grades, and exports. The intro of the video you're watching was edited exactly that way, and I ran every use case live on camera with a timer going so you can see how long each one actually takes.

The whole thing is a folder of files on my computer: a Claude project that knows how to edit. The DaVinci Resolve MCP is the bridge that lets Claude actually drive Resolve, and HyperFrames is the free open-source repo that builds the graphics, it animates them as a web page and records that animation, so you get real motion design instead of a stock template. Both are free on GitHub.

Real numbers from the video. The rough cut took 5 minutes and 41 seconds. I told Claude to do a fully autonomous graphics pass on my intro and take as much time as it needed, and it ran for 1 hour and 38 minutes and came back with something genuinely impressive. I asked for a faster restyle after that and got a better result in about 20 minutes. The color grade, grouping every intro clip and applying a LUT, took about 2 minutes.

I also show you what it can't do. The MCP will group your clips and apply a LUT on its own, but it cannot add nodes or effects on the Color page, so the glow, halation, and grain in my grade got dragged in by hand. It can do that kind of work in Fusion but not in Color, which is silly, and I'd rather you hear it from me than find out an hour in.
