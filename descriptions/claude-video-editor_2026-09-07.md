Summary ⤵️

The intro of this video was edited by Claude in one shot. One prompt, one go. No babysitting, no prompting back and forth, no touching the timeline after. I gave it the raw recording and a background track, told it to edit end to end and not stop until it was finished, and walked away. The prompt is on screen in the intro, and the full unedited recording of the run is linked above so you know I'm not lying. Claude edited the rest of the video too, just not from a single prompt.

Here's the problem with every Claude video editor on the internet, including the one I built months ago: they all lack autonomy. Claude on its own doesn't have the creativity, the taste, or the workflow instructions to turn raw footage into a postable edit. You still sit there for a couple hours giving it direction, which defeats the point.

So instead of relying on Claude to know how to edit, I locked my own style in as presets. One big presets folder: color grade, text animations, zoom-ins and zoom-outs, pop-in and rise-in animations, light and dark backgrounds, the rules for when to build a graphic and how, and the sound effect rules. Every animation is custom built and tuned so it comes out looking good instead of generic AI slop, and Claude never has to think about it.

The pipeline runs like this: raw footage goes to intake, then a rough cut with WhisperX and a recursive sub-agent loop that checks the cut before moving on, then audio polish, color grade, a three-step graphics stage (plan, build, review, looping until the defects are gone), sound effects, a music bed, your review, then a render on the YouTube default preset. A lanes folder holds the instructions for each app: Premiere Pro and DaVinci Resolve are the two that work best, CapCut is meh.

The live run on the intro took almost two and a half hours. I left the result completely raw, no extra refinement, so you can judge how good it actually is. Fair warning: it's really good at talking-head educational YouTube videos. Vlogs and visual-heavy content are harder, and there's no vertical preset for Instagram or TikTok yet.
